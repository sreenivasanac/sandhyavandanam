# Source 2 notes: sandhyavandanam-1.pdf

**What the book is.** "Yajurveda Trikala Sandhyavandanam" ("yajurvEda trikaala sandhyaavandanam"). The subtitle is "आपस्तम्ब & बोधायन सम्प्रदाय / Apastamba And Bodhayana Sampradaya" (p.1). It covers the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda, with the Āpastamba sūtra as the main line and Bodhāyana variants marked (p.17, p.40, p.54+). Each mantra is given in Devanagari (with svara marks), in an ASCII transliteration (capital letters for long vowels and retroflexes, e.g. "kEshava", "praaNaayaama") and with an English italic gloss. The author and publisher are not visible because the front matter is missing from the scan. The book does not say Smārta or Vaiṣṇava. My inference: the illustrations show horizontal stripes (tripuṇḍra-style) on the forehead and upper arms, plus a dot. It also has Vighneśvara dhyānam, a Śiva prārthanā and the abhivādana in "…śarmā" form, while closing with Kṛṣṇa-anusmaraṇa and Keśava/Nārāyaṇa samarpaṇam. That reads as a South Indian Smārta booklet. The Tamil month names "Marghazhi… Karthigai" on p.18 suggest a Tamil-region origin (also inference).

**Page numbering.** The PDF starts at printed book page 15. In these notes, "p.N" means the PDF page index.
- p.1–p.52 = book pages 15–66 (book page = p + 14).
- Book page 67 is missing. p.53–p.65 = book pages 68–80 (book page = p + 15).
- p.66–p.67 = book pages 95–96 (yajñopavīta dhāraṇam). Book pages 1–14 and 81–94 are absent.
- All 67 PDF pages were readable.
- The book's own cross-references sometimes don't match this scan. For example, the gotra-pravara table is cited as "page no. 65 – 80" (p.39), but those book pages hold samidādhānam here. "Rishi tarpanam - page: 26" (p.64) is also wrong.

Overall structure: Part 1 is ācamana through tarpaṇa and ends at p.18 with "iti praatah sandhyaavandanam". Part 2, "sandhyaa-gaayatree-japa-vidhaanam", runs p.19–p.47 and ends "iti trikaala sandhyaagaayatreejapavidhiH". Appendices: samidādhānam (p.48–p.59), brahma-yajña (p.60–p.62), deva-ṛṣi-pitṛ tarpaṇam (p.63–p.65), yajñopavīta dhāraṇam (p.66–p.67).

## 1. Gāyatrī prelude in mārjanam, and ṛṣi/chandas/devatā lines

- Gāyatrī with vyāhṛtis as a prelude before āpo hi ṣṭhā: **absent.** Prokṣaṇam (p.7–p.8) goes straight from line 1 "aapOhiSHTHaa mayObhuvaha" to line 9, then line 10 "Om bhoor-bhuvah-suvaha" at the end, sprinkled around the head. The step before it (p.6–p.7) is the "āpo vā idagṃ sarvam" water prayer with tilak, not a Gāyatrī.
- "āpo hi ṣṭhety ṛcasya sindhudvīpa ṛṣiḥ": **absent** (not at p.7 or p.12).
- "sūryaś cety anuvākasya agnir ṛṣiḥ…" (or the noon/evening equivalents): **absent** (p.9–p.11).
- "dadhikrāvṇṇo … vāmadeva ṛṣiḥ": **absent** at punar-mārjanam (p.12). Vāmadeva appears only for āyātu: "aayaatviti anuvaakasya vaamadEva RiSHihi" (p.26).
- Gāyatrī ṛṣi line: **present** at p.28: "saavitryaa RiSHih-vishvaamitraha | nichRt-gaayatree CHandaha | savitaa dEvataa".
- Praṇava and vyāhṛti ṛṣi lines: **present** at p.24–p.25 (see the table).

## 2. Ācamanam posture and hand

- The text says sitting: "Achamanam is performed in a sitting posture, facing north or east." (p.1)
- The drawing shows squatting on the heels: feet flat on a checkered mat, knees drawn up to the chest, buttocks off the ground. The right hand is cupped at the mouth and the left hand holds the uddharaṇi over the right palm. A plate with a small tumbler sits on the floor to the front right, and the eyes look down into the palm (p.1, and the same figure at p.9–p.11). So text and picture differ: the picture is a kukkuṭāsana-type squat, but the book never uses that word.
- Hand formation: "Fold the forefinger of the right hand and join it with the thumb as shown in the figure. Bend the other three fingers to form space enough for a uddharani (spoon type) full of water." (p.1) The words gokarṇa and māṣa-mātra are not found in source 2. The amount given is "a uddharani … full of water".
- Sips: three, one for each name: "Sip the water from the palm each time uttering the following three mantras." (p.1)
- Touching the body: done as the separate aṅgavandanam (p.2–p.3), with these fingers:
  - Keśava: right cheek, thumb. Nārāyaṇa: left cheek, thumb.
  - Mādhava: right eye, ring finger. Govinda: left eye, ring finger.
  - Viṣṇu: right nostril, index finger. Madhusūdana: left nostril, index finger.
  - Trivikrama: right ear, little finger. Vāmana: left ear, little finger.
  - Śrīdhara: right shoulder, middle finger. Hṛṣīkeśa: left shoulder, middle finger.
  - Padmanābha: navel, all fingers. Dāmodara: head, all fingers.
  - Also: "Make sure the tip of the forefinger touches the thumb while doing the 1st pair … and the thumb touching the respective fingers while doing the remaining pairs." (p.2)
  - Internal inconsistency: the gloss on p.3 says Padmanābha/Dāmodara "touch the heart and the peak of the head", while the table says navel and head.

## 3. Prāṇāyāma (p.4–p.5)

- Fingers: "Fold the index and the middle fingers. Press the right nostril with the thumb and the left with the ring and little fingers." The drawing shows the right hand at the nose with the thumb on the right nostril.
- Order: "Inhale through the left nostril chanting mentally, the first part of the mantra. Hold the breath during the second part and breathe out through the right nostril chanting the third part. This entire process is one 'praanaayaama'".
  - Part 1 is "Om bhoohu" to "Ogum satyam". Part 2 is "Om tat" to "prachOdayaat". Part 3 is "Om-aapO" to "bhoorbhuvassuvarOm".
- Both nostrils closed during the hold: not stated explicitly. My inference is that it is implied, because both nostrils are pressed by the hand position.
- Mental recitation: stated for part 1 ("chanting mentally"). Parts 2 and 3 just say "during" and "chanting".
- After the mantra: "(touch the right ear.)" (p.5, with a drawing of the hand cupped over the right ear).
- Counts: once at each standard point. At 15 (p.25) the heading says "praaNaayaamaha (10 times)", but the note says "(Then do three pranayamas repeating the mantra thrice for each pranayama - thus chanting the mantra nine times.)". The book is internally ambiguous here. After japa: "do pranayama once" (p.31).
- Purpose: the gloss reads the vyāhṛtis as worlds; "Readers keen… can refer to specialised tutors on praanaayaama" (p.5).

## 4. Counts

- **Prokṣaṇam / mārjanam (p.7–p.8)**:
  - Lines 1–7 are sprinkled on the head with the ring finger.
  - Line 8 (yasya kṣayāya jinvatha): "Touch the right and left thighs with the water soaked finger". The drawing labelled 8 shows the man squatting and touching near his feet or the floor, so it doesn't clearly show the thighs.
  - Line 9 is sprinkled on the head again.
  - Line 10 (om bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ): "throw the water circularly in clockwise direction around the head".
- **Punar-mārjanam (p.12–p.13)**: "(Sprinkle water on head after every line)" for dadhikrāvṇṇo … tasmā araṅgamāma vaḥ; "yasya kṣayāya jinvatha … (Sprinkle little water on feet)" (feet here, not thighs); "aapO janayathaa cha naha (Sprinkle water again on head)"; "Om bhoor-bhuvah-suvaha (sprinkle little water around the head)".
- **Arghya (p.13)**: "three times in the morning (stand facing East) and evening (sit facing west) and twice at noon. (stand facing North)". Plus one prāyaścitta arghya ("Repeat arghya pradanam once", p.15) and water circled around the head with "om bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ".
- **Pradakṣiṇa**: only water circled once around the head (p.8, p.13, p.15). Turning the body: in samaṣṭyabhivādanam you turn clockwise through 4 directions and back to the start (p.38). No number of rounds is given.
- **Japa**: 108 (morning), 32 (noon), 64 (evening) (p.30). Finger-line counting: on the right hand, start at the middle line of the ring finger and go clockwise for 10 counts, then anticlockwise back for the next 10; "Thus 108, 32 or 64 gaayatri japa can be counted." A numbered hand drawing shows positions 1–10 (p.30).
- **Tarpaṇa**: one pour per name (p.16), with 9 + 12 names, plus 14 Yama names for Bodhāyana. Appendix deva-ṛṣi-pitṛ tarpaṇa: ṛṣi "pour water twice" (p.63); pitṛ "pour the water (thrice)" (p.65). Deva has no count given.
- **Vighneśvara**: tap the forehead with the knuckles "five times" (p.4).
- **Āvāhana**: three hand rotations, one per name (p.26–p.27).
- **Kṛṣṇa**: "kriSHNa kriSHNa kriSHNa (3 times)" (p.46).

## 5. Standing/sitting and facing direction

- Ācamana: text "sitting… facing north or east" (p.1); the drawing shows a squat.
- Saṅkalpa: seated cross-legged (p.6 drawing).
- Arghya: morning stand facing East; noon stand facing North; evening **sit** facing West (p.13). "raising the heels a little" (p.13). The drawing shows a man standing in profile on a mat, both arms stretched straight forward at shoulder height, hands together, water falling onto a plate at his feet, heels slightly raised.
- Japa: "Sit down to start the japa" (p.21). Facing: morning east, noon east, evening west before sunset and east after (p.30).
- Gāyatrī upasthāna: the drawing shows a man standing and bending forward to touch the floor or mat edge with his finger (p.32). Facing not stated.
- Sūrya upasthāna: morning facing East with folded hands (p.33); noon facing North (p.34); evening facing West (p.36). Standing vs sitting is not stated for these.
- Samaṣṭyabhivādana: turn clockwise through the directions (p.38). Morning starts East; evening starts West.
- Dig-vandanam: E, S, W, N, then "(Facing east)" for above and below (p.40–p.41). The compass diagram with "Morning" and "Evening" arrows is reused (p.40).
- Yama: facing South (p.41). Harihara: facing North (p.42). Narmadā and Sūryanārāyaṇa: East in the morning, West in the evening (p.43–p.44).
- Abhivādana: standing, bending forward with hands over the ears, touching the floor, then a full prostration (p.39 drawing, 3 stages). Kṣamā prārthanā: "touch the floor and prostrate" (p.45).
- Hands:
  - folded for prārthanās (p.21, p.26, p.33, p.39, p.41, p.43)
  - clasped on the right thigh for saṅkalpa (p.6)
  - interlocked above the forehead with a gap to look at the sun at noon (p.35 drawing)
  - for "asāvādityo" (p.15), the drawing shows both hands at the chest with fingers turned toward the body.

## 6. Which hand and fingers

- Ācamana: right palm; forefinger folded to the thumb (p.1).
- Water tilak: ring finger in the uddharaṇi, write "om" on the water, then apply the tilak (p.6–p.7). The drawing shows the ring finger at the forehead.
- Prokṣaṇa: ring finger (p.7). The head circle uses water "into the right palm" (p.8).
- Arghya: both hands; water flows "through the finger tips other than the thumb" (p.13).
- Tarpaṇa (navagraha-Keśavādi): water from the uddharaṇi flows "through the finger tips of right palm" (p.16). The drawing shows the left hand pouring from the spoon into the right palm, held fingers-down over the plate, with the vessel beside it. That is devatīrtha by inference; the book doesn't use the word.
- Appendix tarpaṇa (p.63–p.65):
  - deva: "Hold the upavita with the thumb and pour water down the palm through the tip of fingers pointed downwards"
  - ṛṣi: thread worn like a garland, held "by the right thumb pointing upwards with the other fingers closed", water poured "in the northern side", hand tilted left. For "brahmāṇaṃ svayambhuvam", the water goes "from the palm via the elbow".
  - pitṛ: prācīnāvītī, water "in between the thumb and the index finger while the thumb finger pointing downwards".
- Āsana and Gāyatrī upasthāna: ring finger touches the floor, then the point between the eyebrows (p.22, p.32).
- Nyāsa touches (head, nostrils, chest): the drawings show the right-hand fingertips on the crown, fingers at the nostrils/upper lip, and the palm on the chest (p.24, p.25, p.28).
- Kuśa or pavitram: **not found in source 2** for sandhyā. Darbha appears only as samidādhāna fuel, "Samidha sticks / dry Darba grass" (p.48).

## 7. Props named or shown

- **uddharaṇi** (spoon): named on p.1 ("uddharani (spoon type)"), p.6, p.7, p.9, p.16. Shown in the ācamana and tarpaṇa drawings.
- **Plate**: named p.18 ("pour it down on the plate"), p.63 ("water flows in to the plate"). Shown as a shallow dish on the floor in the ācamana, arghya and tarpaṇa drawings.
- **Pañcapātra**: named only in the yajñopavīta appendix: "left palm should face downwards placed on top of the panchapatra" (p.66). In the sandhyā drawings it appears as a small tumbler next to the plate (p.1, p.16).
- **Āsana / mat**: a checkered mat appears in the ācamana, prāśana, prokṣaṇa (fig. 8) and arghya drawings. Text: "aasanam … pavitram kuru cha aasanam" (p.22). The material is not named.
- **Upper cloth (uttarīya)**: not named. Figures are bare-chested, wearing the yajñopavīta, a bead mala and a lower cloth.
- **Vibhūti / tilaka**: a water tilak (p.6–p.7, p.47). Drawings show horizontal stripes on the forehead and arms; that this is vibhūti is my inference.
- **Water vessel**: the tumbler/pañcapātra in the drawings.
- **Homa kuṇḍa, samidh, rice husk, cow-dung, charcoal**: samidādhāna only (p.48).
- **Hand-diagrams**: the āvāhana palms (3 steps, p.27) and the japa counting hand (p.30).

## 8. Stated purpose or meaning (short quotes)

- Ācamana: "nama-trayee vidya which will cure all diseases" (p.1).
- Aṅgavandana: "This rite strengthens the limbs of the body and shields them" (p.3). Finger/organ seats: "Thumb and mouth are seats of Fire" etc. (p.3).
- Vighneśvara: "meditated upon to keep off all impediments" (p.4).
- Saṅkalpa: "to be worthy of God Parameshvara's grace, by destroying all the sins" (p.6).
- Water tilak: "brings out the importance of water, representing it as the essence of everything" (p.7).
- Prokṣaṇa: "Cleansing of the mind and the body" (p.8).
- Prāśana: "save me from the sins committed through anger" (p.9).
- Prāyaścitta arghya: "Offering of water for belated oblation" (p.14–p.15).
- Asāvādityo: "Meditation on the identity of the self with the supreme being" (p.15).
- Keśavādi tarpaṇa: the 12 names preside over "the twelve months commencing from Margasheersha" (p.18).
- Śiva prārthanā: "ward off evil spirits and hindering elements before starting the 'brahma karma'" (p.20).
- Āvāhana palms: "to invoke and manifest the three forms of Goddess 'Gayatri'" (p.26).
- Upasthāna: "Prayer to Gayatri to return to her seat" (p.32).
- Japasthāna prokṣaṇa: "Raksha – Protection" (p.47).

## 9. Notable points or differences from the usual procedure

1. Order differs. After ācamana + aṅgavandana come Vighneśvara, prāṇāyāma, saṅkalpa, then **jala prārthanā "āpo vā idagṃ sarvam" with a water tilak** (p.6–p.7) before prokṣaṇa. Then prokṣaṇa, prāśana, **ācamana**, punar-mārjana, arghya, prāṇāyāma, prāyaścitta arghya, ātmānusandhāna, ācamana, tarpaṇa, ācamana, and "mayā kṛtam … brahmārpaṇam" ends part 1 (p.18).
2. Mārjana line 8 touches the **thighs** (p.7); in punar-mārjana it is "on feet" (p.12). Line 10 "om bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ" is circled around the head.
3. No ṛṣi/chandas viniyoga lines for mārjana, prāśana or dadhikrāvṇṇo.
4. Evening arghya is done **sitting** facing west (p.13).
5. The prāyaścitta arghya is printed as a normal step, with no "only when late" condition (p.14–p.15). There is no separate turīya arghya.
6. Tarpaṇa is **navagraha + 12 Keśavādi** (+14 Yama names for Bodhāyana), not sandhyā/gāyatrī/sāvitrī/sarasvatī tarpaṇa (p.16–p.18).
7. Japa counts are **108 / 32 / 64**. Evening faces west before sunset and east after (p.30).
8. The japa part opens with an optional Sūryanamaskāra, namo brahmaṇyadevāya, and the apasarpantu Śiva prārthanā (p.19–p.21).
9. Āsana nyāsa uses merupṛṣṭha ṛṣiḥ / atalaṃ chandaḥ / kūrmo devatā; you touch the floor, then the brow, with the ring finger (p.21–p.22). Vighneśvara dhyānam and prāṇāyāma are repeated inside the japa part (p.22–p.23).
10. Prāṇāyāma count is ambiguous at step 15: "10 times" vs "three pranayamas … nine times" (p.25).
11. There is only **one Gāyatrī dhyāna** (five-faced, muktā-vidruma), with no morning/noon/evening forms (p.28–p.29).
12. The āvāhana palm-rotation gesture is "as though looking into the palms … turn or rotate the palms together inwards" (p.26). The upasthāna gesture is "Twisting (locking) both the hands… rotate the clasped palms from inside… and release" (p.31).
13. Noon sūryopasthāna: look at the sun through a gap formed by interlocked hands above the forehead (p.35).
14. The Ṛtaṃ satyam verse is labelled **Harihara vandanam** (p.42).
15. Kṣamā prārthanā repeats abhivādana with prostration and ends "kṛṣṇa kṛṣṇa kṛṣṇa" (p.45–p.46).
16. The final japa-place prokṣaṇa uses **"adyā no deva savitaḥ"**, then the water is poured down and applied as tilak (p.47). This is not the pṛthvi or uttame mantra.
17. Bodhāyana variants are flagged: extra Yama tarpaṇa (p.17); samidādhāna variants (p.52–p.58, e.g. rakṣā "back of neck (Bodhayana)" p.58).
18. Appendices that the app may not need: samidādhānam for brahmacārins (morning facing east, evening facing north, p.48); brahma-yajña "To be performed after Maadhyaahnikam" (p.60); deva-ṛṣi-pitṛ tarpaṇa (p.63–p.65); yajñopavīta (brahmacārī wears one, gṛhastha "two or three", p.66–p.67).
